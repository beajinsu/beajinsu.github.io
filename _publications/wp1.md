Google’s initial public offering (IPO) and Korea’s emissions trading system auctions, both conducted as uniform price auctions, restrict bidders from submitting bids whose total exceeds their available budget. While simple to enforce, this sum-based rule may distort bidding incentives and reduce auction efficiency and revenue. I propose an alternative rule that applies the budget constraint to each individual bid, preserving overall fiscal discipline while allowing greater flexibility in bidding. Using controlled laboratory experiments with private values under both tight and loose budget conditions, I find that the alternative rule improves both efficiency and revenue, with the gains concentrated under tight budget conditions.

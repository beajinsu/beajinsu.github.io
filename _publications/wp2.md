I show that bidder consolidation can raise seller revenue in a multi-unit uniform-price auction with one large bidder and a unit-demand fringe. Consolidating the fringe into equal-capacity blocks replaces its truthful bids with shaded ones, yet those same lower bids make the large bidder less likely to set the price with its own marginal bid, disciplining its pre-existing demand reduction. The net effect is ambiguous in general, but in a benchmark with one rejected bid I identify configurations in which consolidation raises revenue for every distribution within a given regularity class, on a scale from Myerson regularity to a monotone hazard rate. Within that class, the pre- to post-consolidation revenue ratio obeys a sharp upper bound determined only by the number and common capacity of post-consolidation bidders. A bound below one guarantees a revenue gain throughout the class.
